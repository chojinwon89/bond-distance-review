How to read this. A negative Δ means SevenNet under-binds (predicts a weaker/less-negative adsorption energy) relative to DFT; positive means it over-binds. Two headline MAEs are shown per functional: the ±5 eV window value (matches the figure) and the all-point value (includes the extreme outliers). Median |Δ| (medAE) and the fraction within 0.5 eV are robust to those outliers. Coverage is uneven — Au is 51% of all points, Cr has PBE only, and Fe/Co were not run with BEEF-vdW — so cross-metal comparisons are indicative, not perfectly matched.

  • SevenNet-OMNI reproduces dispersion-corrected DFT well. Against BEEF-vdW and PBE+D3 the median error is ~0.26–0.29 eV and ~3 in 4 points land within 0.5 eV, with essentially no systematic bias — the level of agreement AdsorbML/OC20 report for MLIPs.
  • Against bare PBE and r²SCAN it is much worse. r²SCAN carries a large −0.81 eV under-binding bias (R² = −0.53): SevenNet's training target simply is not the r²SCAN surface. If you benchmark this MLIP, compare it to BEEF-vdW / PBE+D3, not to r²SCAN.

  • The coinage/noble metals are solved. Ni, Ag, Cu, Au sit at MAE 0.31–0.68 eV with near-zero bias — SevenNet is production-grade there.
  • Fe, Co, Cr are where it breaks. Pooled over these three, MAE = 2.47 eV (vs 0.63 eV on all non-magnetic metals). On Fe not a single point lands within 0.5 eV and the bias is −4.1 eV; on Cr the bias flips to +2.8 eV. These are the strongly-reactive, high-spin surfaces where the ML potential-energy surface diverges from DFT.
  • It is not "magnetism" per se — it is Fe/Co/Cr. Ni is ferromagnetic and yet the single best metal in the set. So the failure tracks the strong, spin-polarised bonding of Fe/Co/Cr rather than magnetism as a label — exactly the regime the magnetic-surface-aware GOAD work targets.
  • Pt and Pd are a quieter, separate problem. Their MAE is modest but every point under-binds (bias −0.4 to −1.0 eV across all four functionals, pooled R² < −1): a consistent offset, not scatter — a candidate for a simple per-metal correction.

Small inorganic probes and saturated/unsaturated hydrocarbons are easiest (MAE ~0.6–0.7 eV); oxygenates that bind through carbonyl or carboxyl groups — ketones, carboxylic and hydroxy/keto acids, esters/lactones — are the hardest (MAE ~1.0–1.2 eV, only ~40% within 0.5 eV).

6 · What this means

  • Use a dispersion-corrected reference. SevenNet-OMNI matches BEEF-vdW (MAE 0.40 eV, zero bias) and PBE+D3 (medAE 0.26 eV) well; treat its numbers as approximating those, not PBE or r²SCAN.
  • Trust it on Ni/Ag/Cu/Au + small hydrocarbons & inorganics; distrust it on Fe/Co/Cr and on large multi-carbonyl oxygenates. Those two axes — magnetic strong-binders and big O-rich molecules — stack: the very-worst systems are Fe/Co/Cr with an adsorbate, or large Au·polyol/acid.
  • The failures are directional and structured, not random. Fe/Co under-bind, Cr over-binds, Pt/Pd carry a flat −0.4–1.0 eV offset. Structured error is correctable — per-metal offsets for Pt/Pd, and a spin/geometry-aware refiner for Fe/Co/Cr (the GOAD hybrid explorer/refiner route).
  • Caveat — these are single points. DFT and ML share one geometry, so a ±8 eV gap means the ML-relaxed structure sits where DFT sees strong repulsion (or spurious binding) — most acute on magnetic surfaces. It reflects a wrong ML minimum, not necessarily smooth energy noise.
  • Caveat — coverage. Au is 51% of the data; Cr is PBE-only and Fe/Co lack BEEF-vdW, so their pooled MAEs mix functionals. Filling Fe/Co/Cr × BEEF-vdW/PBE+D3 would firm up the magnetic story.
Molecule-family rule. Each of the 123 molecules is assigned to exactly one family by first-match priority: inorganic/small probe → hydroxy/keto acid → carboxylic acid → ester/ether/lactone → ketone → aldehyde → alcohol/polyol → aromatic → alkene → alkane. So aromatic aldehydes (furfural, vanillin) count as aldehydes, phenols as aromatics, and lactones as esters/ethers. Metrics: MAE=mean |Δ|, medAE=median |Δ|, bias=mean Δ (ML−DFT), R² for predicting DFT from ML. Source data: dft_singlepoint_vs_sevennet.csv.
