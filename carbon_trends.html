What the carbon grouping shows

  • Detachment concentrates at C0–C1. The one/zero-carbon strong binders (CO, CO₂, H₂O, NO, O₂, formate…) have the highest detached rate — exactly the species that must chemisorb.
  • Mid-size (C2–C7) look healthiest — 55–66% land in the bonded range.
  • Large molecules (C8+) drift to “borderline” — a partly geometric effect (a big flat molecule’s closest heavy atom is naturally ~3–3.6 Å), not necessarily a failure.
  • Metal class dominates carbon number. Splitting by magnetism (panel 3) shows the magnetic 3d metals Cr/Fe/Co/Ni are offset upward by ~1.5–2 Å at every carbon count. Most foundation MLIPs carry no spin/magnetic degrees of freedom, a known weak spot on magnetic surfaces — a plausible cause of the systematic under-binding in the 198 flagged failures. (These metals are also the most oxophilic/reactive, so geometry alone can’t fully separate magnetism from reactivity.)
