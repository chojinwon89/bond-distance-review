How this differs from the single-point page. There, DFT and ML score the same geometry, isolating the energy model. Here, DFT and GOAD+MLIP each run their own relaxation and we compare the resulting adsorption energies — the realistic end-to-end test of the workflow. A negative Δ means GOAD+MLIP predicts a stronger (more negative) binding than DFT (over-binds); positive means it under-binds. The overlap is small (Cu/Pd/Pt × CO2/ethane/ethene/ethanol), and functional coverage is uneven — PBE has 21 pairs while BEEF-vdW has 10 — so R² on these narrow energy ranges is noisy and MAE/bias are the reliable metrics.

4 · Takeaways

  • The GOAD+MLIP workflow reproduces dispersion-corrected DFT adsorption energies well. Against BEEF-vdW the end-to-end MAE is 0.14 eV and against PBE+D3 it is 0.18 eV, with 95% of all 58 pairs inside 0.5 eV — competitive with the single-point energy-only agreement.
  • The apparent PBE disagreement is physics, not an MLIP failure. The uniform −0.36 eV offset vs bare PBE is the dispersion contribution SevenNet-OMNI includes and PBE omits; it disappears once you compare to a vdW-corrected functional.
  • Metals are all in good shape. Cu is essentially unbiased (MAE 0.21 eV); Pd/Pt show a small −0.2 eV over-binding but still keep ≥95% of points within 0.5 eV.
  • Caveat — small, uneven overlap. Only 21 systems (Cu/Pd/Pt × four C1–C2 molecules) have both a converged DFT relaxation and a matching GOAD+MLIP structure. Extending the DFT set to more surfaces/molecules would sharpen these numbers; the per-molecule CO2 row in particular rests on only 5 pairs.
